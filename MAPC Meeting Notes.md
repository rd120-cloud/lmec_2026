Based it off of a study out of Chicago, DuPaul housing studies initiative
submarkets analysis. Part of a network that MAPC is a part of. Started by
looking at what they did. Tweaked the methods from that study.

Produced numerous different submarkets clusters based off different clustering
methods.

Based on census tracts. Some of the data is municipal, but its all standardized
to 2010 census tracts. Included home sale transactions purchased from the Warren
Group (?). The Chicago study included more demographic data, while this study
was more focused on the inventory and the market. Demographic information was an
overlay.

There was some heuristic work to determine how many submarkets make sense. The
clustering analysis can make as many clusters as you tell it too. They had an
extra step to determine the true differences between submarkets.

They predict there will be fewer submarkets the further back you go, because
there is less variance in the region and a much smaller urban center going back
that far. The urban structure was less developed, and the types of density were
more obvious back then.

Some differences are very clear, like how submarket 3 is mostly triple deckers.

Change variables were very important to them for planning purposes. This encodes
things like development, gentrification, etc. Investor indicators and cash sales
also show market qualities with particular policy importance. This is also why
demographics were not super vital, because that was downstream of the market
itself. Market interactions are more "results".
